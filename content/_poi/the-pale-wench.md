# The Pale Wench

*Smuggling Brigantine of the Black Coast*

The **Pale Wench** is a lean, fast brigantine built for speed, concealment, and quiet departures. She is not a warship, but neither is she defenseless. Designed for coastal trade along the dim waters of Mordt, the ship has been extensively modified over the years to support smuggling operations. Her hull is narrow and deep-keeled, allowing her to cut quickly through rough water and slip into shallow coves where larger imperial vessels cannot follow.

Her name comes from the ship’s **ghost-pale hull planking**, bleached almost bone-white by years of salt and sea wind. In darkness she appears faint and colorless against the water, a trait Captain Veylan intentionally maintains by periodically scraping and bleaching the outer boards rather than painting them.

The Pale Wench is crewed by roughly **16 sailors**, though she can operate with fewer in emergencies. Max capacity rests closer to 20.

## Ship Specifications

**Type:** Brigantine<br>
**Length:** ~85ft<br>
**Beam (Widest Point):** ~24ft<br>
**Crew Capacity:** 20<br>

## Armament

The ship is not heavily armed but carries enough weaponry to discourage pursuit.

### Forward Deck Cannon
*Mounted on a reinforced swivel base near the bow.*<br><br>
**Capabilities:**
* Can be rotated about 40 degrees left or right
* Used primarily to **disable pursuit vessels or destroy rudders**.
* Reload time is slow unless multiple crew assist.

### Deck Ballista
*Mounted near the stern rail.*<br><br>
**Capabilities:**
* Fires heavy bolts
* Launches rope lines
* Harpoons small boats
Often used in chases to snag enemy rigging.

## Exterior Features

### Hull

<div class="detail-indent">The hull planking is pale driftwood grey, streaked with salt stains and darker pitch seams. Reinforced ribs run along the lower hull, suggesting the ship has been repaired many times. The bow curves upward into a narrow prow designed to cut water efficiently. Hidden along the waterline are **three disguised cargo hatches** used for covert loading during night operations.</div>

### Foremast

<div class="detail-indent">Tallest mast. Carries square sails designed for strong forward wind. Used for rapid acceleration when fleeing.</div>

### Mainmast

<div class="detail-indent">Slightly shorter but heavier. Carries a <b>large fore-and-aft sail</b> that allows tight maneuvering and angled sailing against the wind.</div>

### Sail Cloth

<div class="detail-indent">The sails are dyed a <b>dirty grey-green</b>, helping the ship blend into fog and darkness. In bright lantern light they appear faded and tattered, though they are carefully maintained.</div>

### Figurehead

<div class="detail-indent">The ship’s figurehead is a <b>weathered carved woman</b>, pale-faced and expressionless, hair swept back as though blown by wind. Her eyes are hollowed cavities. Crew members refer to the figurehead simply as <b>“The Wench.”</b> Ship superstition claims the ship sails faster when the figurehead is washed in seawater before departure.</div>

### Flag

<div class="detail-indent">The Pale Wench flies a <b>charcoal-black triangular flag</b> bearing a simple symbol: A <b>pale handprint</b> centered on the cloth. This is in-part a <b>smuggler signal</b> recognized by certain coastal networks; the mark indicates neutrality among smugglers and signals that the vessel trades information as often as goods. When approaching lawful ports, the ship instead flies a <b>faded merchant pennant</b> belonging to a long-defunct coastal trading company.</div>

### Rigging

<div class="detail-indent">The rigging is dense but efficiently arranged. Two key features are <b>Quick-release sail lines</b> for rapid maneuvering, and a <b>Cargo hoist system</b> used for lifting contraband crates aboard quickly. Experienced sailors can adjust the ship’s full sail configuration in under a minute.</div>


## Deck Layout

### Bow

<div class="detail-indent">The bow contains the ship’s forward cannon mount, positioned to fire ahead or slightly to either side of the vessel. Just behind it sits the anchor winch used to raise and lower the ship’s anchor. At the very front is the platform supporting the ship’s figurehead.</div>

### Main Deck

<div class="detail-indent">The main deck serves as the ship’s primary working area. Cargo hatches provide access to the hold below, while rigging access points and sail control lines allow the crew to manage the sails. Several lantern posts are positioned along the deck for illumination during night operations.</div>

### Stern Deck (Quarterdeck)

<div class="detail-indent">The stern deck, or quarterdeck, is slightly elevated above the main deck and serves as the command area of the ship. It houses the helm used to steer the vessel along with basic navigation instruments used by the captain and crew during voyages.</div>


## Interior 
Below deck the Pale Wench contains **two primary levels** plus the hidden smuggling space.

### Captain’s Quarters

{% capture captain %}

The captain’s quarters are located at the stern of the ship beneath the quarterdeck. The room is modest but orderly, centered around a broad navigation table covered with sea charts pinned down by brass tacks. A narrow bed is built into the wall to conserve space, and small stern windows look out over the ship’s wake. A lockable chest holds documents and navigational materials. Captain Veylan also keeps a rack of ornate swords, a pair of signal lanterns, and a brass spyglass in the room.

### Bosun’s Cabin

{% capture bosun %}

The bosun’s cabin sits near the crew quarters so the bosun can remain close to the sailors they supervise. The room contains racks of tools, rope repair kits, sailcloth patches, and spare rigging hooks used for ship maintenance. A small cot occupies one corner for resting between watches. The cabin typically smells of tar, rope oil, and damp wood from the constant work of maintaining the vessel.

### Quartermaster’s Cabin

{% capture quarter %}

The quartermaster’s cabin is positioned beside the cargo hold and functions as the ship’s logistical center. From here the quartermaster tracks supplies, rations, and cargo manifests. The room contains a locked ledger chest for records, a set of scales used to weigh trade goods, sealed crate racks, and ration lockers for crew provisions. The quartermaster also controls access to the hidden smuggling level beneath the cargo hold, opening the concealed floor panels when contraband needs to be stored or retrieved.

### The Smuggler’s Level (Hidden Compartment)


{% capture smuggle %}
Beneath the cargo hold lies a **concealed crawlspace deck**, accessible through a disguised trap panel beneath stacked cargo. The entrance is cleverly hidden using **false floor planks** and heavy barrels that must be shifted aside.

#### Interior of the Smuggler’s Level

Low ceiling — most people must crouch. Reinforced beams support the false floor above.

**Features:**

* Concealed crate racks
* Waterproof storage boxes
* Padded compartments for fragile goods

The space can hold **several tons of hidden cargo** without detection during inspections.

## Crew

**16 total**, including the captain. Lean crew, experienced, reliant on trust and routine rather than numbers.
