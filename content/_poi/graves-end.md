Grave's End is a fortified frontier town on the northern coast of Palor, built along towering sea cliffs where the forests of the Outer Provinces meet the sea. It is the last larger settlement before the untamed interior, a place where travelers gather before venturing into the dangerous wilderness beyond.

Unlike the grand cities of the Inner Provinces, Grave's End was built for survival rather than prosperity. Its stone walls, timber structures, and narrow cliffside streets have been rebuilt countless times after storms, raids, fires, and disasters. Every building bears evidence of the town's long struggle against the elements and the dangers beyond its borders.

Though officially under Imperial rule, the Empire's influence weakens significantly this far from the Inner Provinces. Grave's End has become a refuge for smugglers, escaped prisoners, failed adventurers, displaced families, and those seeking a life beyond Imperial scrutiny. The town's ports remain one of the safer routes for moving goods and people unnoticed.

**The recent attack by the Prophet** has left portions of the town scarred. Entire sections of the upper districts suffered fire damage, and smoke still hangs over collapsed rooftops. Reconstruction has begun, but the people of Grave's End are familiar with rebuilding. For many residents, this is simply another disaster the town must endure.

## Overview

**Population:** ~2,400 permanent residents.

**Demographics:** Predominantly Tiefling, with a significant Human population.

**Government:** Local council overseen by civic leaders, though Imperial officials maintain a presence.

**Economy:** Grave's End survives through persistence.

* Coastal fishing
* Lumber harvesting
* Stone quarrying
* Cured fish
* Caravan trade
* Smuggling and unofficial trade routes

Traders from the Inner Provinces occasionally arrive with manufactured goods, though supply is inconsistent due to the town's remote location.

### Features

Grave's End is divided into several distinct districts built vertically along the cliffs.

#### The Docks

The lowest level of Grave's End is carved directly into the rocky coastline.

Stone quays and reinforced seawalls protect the harbor from the violent tides of the Greywater Sea. Fishing vessels, ferries, smugglers, and merchant ships all make use of the docks, though much of the traffic operates quietly beneath Imperial oversight.

The broad stone platforms here also serve as a refuge during major fires, offering open space and immediate access to the sea.

#### The Central District

The heart of Grave's End.

Built around the main cliffside roads, the Central District contains the market square, town hall, workshops, inns, shrines, and most public services.

The Central District is where most visitors first experience Grave's End: crowded, practical, and constantly in motion.

#### Hollow Vein District

The eastern quarter of the city.

Originally built around ancient human ruins buried beneath the cliffs, Hollow Vein has become home to miners, laborers, refugees, former prisoners, and families unable to afford the safer districts.

The streets are narrow and uneven, with cramped homes built around forgotten stone foundations and collapsed tunnels. 

**The party earned considerable goodwill here after treating the sick and injured.**

#### Northern Gate

The northern edge of Grave's End opens toward the forests of Palor.

Heavy timber barricades, stone watchposts, and reinforced gates protect the road leading inland. Beyond these walls, the wilderness becomes increasingly dangerous, with corrupted forests, hostile creatures, and abandoned settlements scattered throughout the region.

The Northern Gate is often the final checkpoint before expeditions leave civilization behind.

### Architecture

Grave's End reflects centuries of rebuilding.

Most structures are constructed from rough-cut grey stone reinforced with heavy timber framing. Slate roofs, narrow windows, thick walls, and deep foundations protect buildings from coastal storms and harsh weather.

The streets are steep, winding, and irregular, following the natural shape of the cliffs rather than any planned design.

Throughout the town are remnants of the ancient human civilization that once occupied the region. Forgotten stairways descend beneath modern buildings, old foundations emerge from cellar walls, and strange stonework occasionally appears after landslides or collapsed streets.

Most residents have grown accustomed to these ruins. They are simply part of life in Grave's End.

## Atmosphere

Despite its name, Grave's End is not a place of despair.

It is a town built by people who have endured more than they should have.

Children still play along the cliffside streets. Fishermen repair nets beside damaged piers. Lanterns continue glowing through smoke-filled nights. Neighbors rebuild homes that have fallen before and will likely fall again.

Grave's End represents the Outer Provinces themselves: battered, stubborn, and alive.
