## The Research Chamber
*Play "Alien", by Joel Nielsen*
{: .notice--info}

**Read Aloud:**
*The channel narrows, then opens into a chamber humming with unnatural resonance. At its center lies a makeshift Guild research station; a large crystal overturned as used as a table, scrawled papers and an open journal, and crystalline shards glimmering faintly under the chamber’s glow. One wall is marked with chalk and sigils — the remnants of a psychic ward circle, its lines half-erased, unpowered. In the center, a strange device sits, with charred power crystal lying in pieces at its base, drained of all energy.*

**Findings:**

- **Arcana DC 12:** Recognize the ward circle as the same type used at Camp Alpha, once powered to shield against telepathic intrusion. Without energy, it is useless.
- **Arcana DC 15:** Note that the ward’s failure came from overuse rather than sabotage. It held long enough for extended work — Sybil’s last attempt at getting the message out..

- **Sybil Echo Stones**
	
	- *Collected data indicates Ebb and crystalline phases are co-dependent states within a singular ecosystemic cycle. Opposition between them is misinterpretation; they are sequential, not adversarial.*
	- *Increased proximity to crystalline formations appears to affect behavior. Talis reports auditory impressions during rest cycles; Brannich demonstrates heightened vigilance, bordering on agitation. Both have spent extended time near exposed shards. No further direct experimentation has been sanctioned — risk of exposure is too great. Recommend stricter rotation schedules near the formations to limit prolonged exposure.*
	- *The ward won't hold for much longer. Power reserves are depleted, and no viable replacements remain. My team is... gone -breaks down- My notes are here -deep breath- I think — and the relay should carry enough to inform the Guild. If...this is my last record: what we found here wasn't attacking us, but trying to protect itself. We interrupted a natural cycle- a rhythm older than...I don't know- one we disrupted. If... I don't make it, maybe those that come after will understand, and keep a safe distance...*

This chamber is the pivot point: players must choose whether to finish Sybil’s work, destroy the Crown’s core, or light the beacon and hold out until rescue arrives.

## Option 1: **Communing with the Crown**

This is the “understanding” route, where players risk exposure to speak with a godlike hive-mind.

**How it Sounds:**

* The Crown doesn’t speak in words, but in overlapping impressions: weight, pressure, the sound of stone fracturing. When it “replies,” players might hear their own voices echoed back, fractured and delayed.
* Tone is not hostile. It is bewildered, almost childlike, and *immensely ancient*. It tries to explain the cycle of Ebb → Crown → Ebb as inevitable, and that the Guild’s interference accelerated the shift unnaturally.

**Skill Challenge (Negotiation):**

* PCs must make **three successful checks** before two failures.
* Relevant checks:

  * **Persuasion (DC 16):** Convince the Crown the Guild isn’t a threat.
  * **Arcana (DC 14):** Frame your argument in terms of magical cycles and resonance.
  * **Insight (DC 14):** Sense when the Crown is misunderstanding and reframe.
  * **Religion/Nature (DC 14):** Contextualize the cycle as natural, not war.
* **Failure:** The Crown sees the party as *fragments of a larger whole* and tries to assimilate them (all PCs gain 1 tick of Resonance).
* **Success:** The Crown calms, letting the PCs leave peacefully. It grants them safe passage out, but not before implanting one last vision of the endless cycle.

## Option 2: **Destroying the Crown**

This isn’t about smashing it with swords — it’s about disrupting its resonance long enough to collapse the crystalline structure.

**Stage 1 – Shattering the Core:**

* The party must find the Crown’s pulsating nexus (a massive crystalline heart, thrumming like a drumbeat).
* **Skill Challenge:** Three stages of “disruption.”

  * **Arcana/Religion (DC 16):** Overload resonance with magical feedback.
  * **Athletics (DC 15):** Pry open shielding plates of hardened crystal.
  * **Investigation (DC 14):** Spot the “fault lines” where resonance is weakest.
* Each stage triggers waves of Crown defenders. Suggested encounters:

  * **Crystalline Hybrids (former Guild team).**
  * **Crystal Golems (stone golem reskinned with shard-like bodies).**

**Stage 2 – Collapse:**

* The structure begins to cave in as resonance destabilizes. The party must escape through falling crystal, collapsing ooze tunnels, and psychic backlash.
* **Skill Challenge:** DC 14, group success required. Acrobatics, Athletics, and Constitution saves to withstand falling debris and suffocating crystal dust.

**Escape Result:**

* If successful, the structure collapses entirely, leaving only ooze where it once stood.
* If they fail, one or more PCs are “left behind,” consumed as the Crown detonates.

## Option 3: **Escaping with the Relay**

This is the “survive and bring back data” route.

**The Relay:**

* The unpowered relay sits in Sybil’s research chamber. By default, it lacks the energy to open a return gate.
* 
* **Methods to Power It:**
  1. **Guild Crystals (from Alpha Camp):** If the party preserved them, they can slot them in for a clean activation.
  2. **The Psychic Resonator (Sybil’s Prototype):** Half-built, unstable, requires Arcana/Engineering checks to complete (DC 16). Success powers the relay; failure causes a pulse of Resonance (ticks to all PCs).
  3. **Drawing from the Crown Itself:** Attuning to its energy to power the relay. This requires one PC to voluntarily commune and siphon its resonance — they must succeed on **3 Wisdom saves (DC 15)** or risk full assimilation.

**Final Stand:**

* Once powered, the relay takes **5 rounds to fully charge.**
* During this time, crystalline hybrids and guardians converge on the chamber, leading to a **final defensive battle.**
* If the party holds the relay until it fires, they escape back to Sen — data in hand.

✨ **DM Note on Design:**

* *Communing* rewards roleplay-heavy groups.
* *Destroying* rewards combat/puzzle-heavy groups.
* *Escaping* creates a tense “defend the point” finale that mixes both.
  Each is equally valid, but all should feel like a **hard choice under pressure**.
