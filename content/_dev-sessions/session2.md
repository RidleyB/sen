## Collapse into the Caves

Beyond the raised guard post, the corridor narrows sharply. Chunks of masonry and jagged beams form a jagged barricade, the walls leaning like they might fall at any moment. The air is thick, musty, and heavy with dust and smoke. You can feel the faint tremor of the earlier explosion in your bones, a persistent hum of instability.

As you step forward, the floor gives way beneath you. Stone and rubble tumble away. You lurch, catching yourself on a broken beam, and realize that the prison itself has fractured — the walls ahead are gone entirely, opening into a **dark cavity below**.

Cold, damp air rushes upward, carrying the scent of earth and mineral, a stark contrast to the iron and rot of the prison. Water trickles faintly from somewhere unseen, echoing through the chamber with an irregular drip.

Jagged stone surrounds you on all sides, forming a natural chute leading down into a **subterranean cave system**. Old mining tracks, half-collapsed wooden supports, and abandoned tools hint that this area was once used by laborers long before the prison was built. Shadows twist and stretch in the dim light, making shapes that may or may not be alive.

Faint sounds reach you: the scrape of claw on stone, the drip of water into pools, the distant echo of collapsing tunnels further down. Somewhere below, the faint glimmer of phosphorescent mineral catches your eye, reflecting off slick rock like distant stars.

You realize this cave system could serve as both a **way out of the prison** and a potential **dangerous labyrinth**, filled with unstable rock, starving beastmen, and the remnants of abandoned mines.

**Narrative Hooks / Environmental Features**

* **Cave Path**: Twisting tunnels, occasional loose rocks that might fall if stepped on or disturbed.
* **Abandoned Mining Gear**: Rusted pickaxes, wheelbarrows, tracks that may still hold remnants of carts — could be used as improvised weapons or tools.
* **Water & Light**: Dripping pools and faintly glowing minerals can provide both illumination and hazards (slippery floors).
* **Ambient Sounds**: Echoes of collapsing stone, distant growls, and the whisper of unseen creatures heighten tension.
* **Escape & Exploration Choice**: Players can move carefully to avoid collapse, scout for easier paths, or rush and risk alerting nearby threats.

Here’s how you can narrate and structure this section of the dungeon based on your map and goals. The flow moves naturally from the **collapsed entry point (bottom right)** toward the **coastal outflow (top left)**, weaving in tension, opportunity, and narrative reinforcement.

## Into the Mines

The air grows cooler and wetter as you descend the fractured tunnel. Your boots scrape over broken stone and twisted iron, the remnants of old prison infrastructure jutting from the earth like ribs. Drips echo faintly in the dark, mingling with the slow pulse of water flowing somewhere nearby.

The flicker of light from your torches reveals a network of **rough-hewn tunnels**, clearly **once mined** but long since reclaimed by the dark. You can make out **carved supports**, **rusted rails**, and a **thin channel of greenish water** flowing through one side — **the mine runoff** that snakes deeper into the rock.

---

## Branching Paths

As the party progresses, they come upon a **junction of caverns** — several branching channels twisting away from the main runoff.

* The **central path** follows the **runoff**, winding slowly **upward** toward fresher air — the **way out**.
* The **side paths** descend further into **dark, uncharted tunnels**, where the echoes are deeper and the air turns stale.
* Small bones, scraps of fur, and claw marks along the stone walls suggest the **beastmen** prowl the lower tunnels.

If players investigate or wander off the main path, they’ll sense:

The faint sound of breathing. Wet. Animal.
The scrape of bone on stone.

It’s clear that **further in means danger**, while **following the runoff** feels faintly cleaner, fresher — a lifeline toward freedom.

---

## Beastmen Ambush (Avoidable Encounter)

As you pass through a narrow cavern, your light catches the glint of eyes — a low, guttural hiss breaks the silence.

Three **Beastmen scavengers** lurk near the runoff, **feeding on a corpse** or **gnawing on bones** from old miners.

* If the players **move quietly** (DC 13 Stealth), they can **slip by unseen** — the creatures too distracted by their meal.
* A failed check draws their attention: snarling, they rise, bone weapons in hand, scrambling over the rocks.
* If engaged, the fight is close and claustrophobic; the uneven terrain and narrow space make movement limited.

**Stats:** use **Gnoll Witherlings** or **custom Beastmen** with 11 AC, 9 HP, Multiattack (bite/claw).

Optional Kael’ith interference — a faint *crack of blue energy* deeper in the tunnels, startling the beasts mid-fight, giving the players an opening to flee.


## The Supply Room (Rest Point)

> The runoff leads you to a low, rectangular chamber, half-collapsed but mostly intact.
> Rusted lanterns hang from the ceiling. Broken crates and barrels line the walls. It smells of old oil, mildew, and rope — a forgotten supply cache, abandoned decades ago.

Inside are **mouldering supplies** and **old guard stores**:

## Cave Outflow

> Beyond the supply room, the runoff widens into a jagged tunnel. The walls glisten with green light reflecting off the water.
> The sound of waves grows faintly audible now — a rhythmic pulse beyond the stone. The smell of salt creeps in.

As the party pushes on:

* The air grows cleaner, colder.
* Beastmen tracks thin out.
* Broken beams and rusted rails give way to rough, natural stone.

At the tunnel’s end, a final climb — slick and steep — opens into the **coastal outflow**, where daylight filters through cracks in the stone.

> You emerge to the sound of distant surf — pale light bleeding through a ragged hole in the cliffside. The runoff pours out in a green stream into the sea below. The air tastes like salt and freedom.


Here’s the scene you can read or paraphrase for your players, clean and direct, no fluff padding — just strong sensory description and actionable detail.

## Arrival at Kael’ith’s Safehouse (Session End)

The long climb through the runoff tunnels ends at a break in the stone where cold, salt-heavy wind cuts through the air. You push out through a narrow grate bent by the explosion’s shockwave. Beyond it, the coast opens wide — jagged black cliffs, the distant crash of waves, and the lights of a small port flickering a few kilometers north.

The place is quiet. Cargo cranes sit motionless. Lanterns sway. Even from here you can hear gulls and the low rumble of longshoremen finishing their shifts.

Kael’ith leads you along the cliff edge, keeping low, stopping whenever the wind picks up or patrol torches sweep across the dark. Eventually he brings you to a squat shack half-buried in the stone. Driftwood patched the cracks in the walls. A rusted chimney leans like it’s given up.

He pushes the door open.

Inside: a single room. A battered table. A cold stove. A cot that’s seen too many damp nights. Shelves stacked with loose bandages, salted fish, a few tools, and a worn cloak or two — nothing valuable, everything functional. The smell of sea brine and old oil clings to everything. Kael’ith shuts the door behind you.

He lifts a few floorboard back to reveal his true supplies; weapons, armor, enough to get started.

“You’ll be safe here,” he says. “For tonight. Patrols don’t sweep this stretch often.”

He drops to a knee beside Riot, checking his breathing.

“I’ll keep watch. Rest while you can. Tomorrow, we talk about getting you off this island.”

Outside, the wind batters the boards. Inside, for the first time since the scaffold, the world stops trying to kill you.

**Session ends.**
