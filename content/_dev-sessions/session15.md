# Session 15 — The Wound Answers

## Session Purpose

This session follows the party’s first true victory against the Black Blood: the cleansing of the font beneath Haldrath.

The session should do four things:

1. Let the party survive the immediate consequence of the miracle.
2. Introduce Corrin properly.
3. Establish the complicated emotional history between Riot and Kael’ith.
4. Bring the Prophet onto the stage without making him fightable yet.

The simplified city structure is:

* **Hollow Vein Ward:** the Ashborn heretics interrogate people the party healed.
* **Central Grave’s End:** the Prophet publicly murders Fenrur and begins the main encounter.

---

# DM Truth

The Prophet felt Haldrath go silent.

The Imar Wound beneath Haldrath was not just a source of Black Blood to him. It was proof. It proved that the world was rotten beyond saving. It proved that his doctrine was right.

Then Zeraph’s lantern changed Black Blood into clean water.

To the Prophet, this is not merely a threat.

It is blasphemy.

It suggests that the world may not need to burn.

The Prophet attacks Grave’s End because he believes whoever cleansed Haldrath returned there. He does not know who did it yet. He intends to find out.

---

## The Party Knows

* They cleansed the font beneath Haldrath.
* Black Blood became clean water.
* They were told Kael’ith and Riot were going to a rebel contact named Corrin in Grave’s End.
* Riot’s return from prison is still not public.
* They previously healed many people in Hollow Vein Ward.

## Corrin Knows

* Kael’ith and Riot needed shelter.
* The party helped them escape Karthold.
* Riot must remain hidden for now.
* The city is unstable.
* The rebels are splintered.

Corrin does **not** know what happened beneath Haldrath unless the party tells them.

## The Prophet Knows

* Haldrath’s wound went silent.
* Something purified Black Blood.
* The responsible people likely returned to Grave’s End.
* Fenrur spoke to outsiders recently.
* Hollow Vein patients were healed by outsiders recently.

The Prophet does **not** know Riot is alive.

# Main NPCs

## Corrin

Rebel contact in Grave’s End.

Use they/them unless you already have a preference.

Corrin is practical, guarded, and hard to impress. They are not a dreamer. They are the kind of rebel who keeps people alive after idealists make dangerous promises.

Role in session:

* Introduce the party to the current state of Grave’s End.
* Shelter them for the long rest.
* Protect Riot’s secret.
* Become the party’s main rebel contact in the city.

Suggested manner:

* Dry humor.
* Always watching exits.
* Does not ask questions twice.
* Hates dramatic people but keeps saving them.

## Riot

Male.

Former de facto head of the rebellion. Not a perfect leader, but charismatic, idealistic, and capable enough to move people.

He was imprisoned in Karthold. His imprisonment shattered rebel momentum and helped splinter the movement.

During the escape from Karthold, Riot lost his leg in an explosion. He is still alive, but he no longer believes he is capable of being what people need.

Important visual:

Riot has tattoos on his fists.

One says **Riot**.

The other says **Ruin**.

The second is for Kael’ith’s old name.

Role in session:

* Show his self-doubt.
* Show his bond with Kael’ith.
* Show that he still wants to help, even if he does not believe he can lead.
* Keep him hidden from the public.

## Kael’ith

Male.

Once a regular Tiefling. In those days, he named himself **Ruin**.

He and Riot were inseparable. They fought hand in hand, idealistic and headstrong, trying to shape the world into something better.

As the rebellion became real and people around them died, Kael’ith chose a terrible path. He infiltrated the Nimyrians through Ascension. Riot saw it as betrayal. Kael’ith saw it as the only way to turn the tide.

After Kael’ith ascended, he and Riot were separated. Without Kael’ith beside him, Riot spiraled deeper into danger. A few years later, Riot was imprisoned.

Kael’ith later tried to break Riot out of Karthold. It went badly. Riot lost his leg. The party became tied to both of them through the escape.

Role in session:

* Protect Riot at all costs.
* Feel guilt without confessing it directly.
* Want Riot to become a symbol again, but fear what returning too early will do.
* Begin laying groundwork for his eventual sacrifice.

## Fenrur

Non-heretical Ashborn.

Fenrur represents the older Ashborn tradition: ash as memory, mourning, and warning. The Prophet’s heretics represent ash as purification, judgment, and future.

Fenrur spoke with the party previously.

The Prophet murders Fenrur publicly this session to show the difference between old Ashborn and heretical Ashborn.

Role in session:

* Show that not all Ashborn follow the Prophet.
* Become the Prophet’s introductory victim.
* Give the party one final clue before or during his death.

# Act I — The Flood Beneath Haldrath

Begin immediately after the font turns to water.

The party has not rested.

One party member is unconscious.

## Read-Aloud

The black ichor recoils from the lantern’s light.

For one impossible breath, the cavern stills.

Then the font breaks open.

Clear water erupts from the wound in the earth, violent and pure, crashing across the stone in a white surge. It tears dead briar from the walls. It strips black rot from the floor. The stink of old blood vanishes beneath the cold scent of mineral water and ancient earth.

Then the tunnel behind you groans.

A crack races up the cavern wall.

The water is rising.

---

# Skill Challenge — The Rising Water

## Goal

Escape Haldrath before the tunnels fully flood.

## Difficulty

**6 successes before 3 failures.**

Default DC: **13**

Use DC 15 for especially dangerous or improvised actions.

At 6 successes, the party escapes in control.

At 3 failures, they are swept through part of the ruin and escape with consequences.

---

## The Unconscious Party Member

Ask immediately:

**“Who has them?”**

One conscious PC must carry, drag, support, or protect the unconscious character.

### If the Unconscious PC Is at 0 HP

Before the challenge begins, the party must stabilize them.

Options:

* Medicine DC 10
* Spare the Dying
* Any magical healing
* Potion or other stabilizing effect

Once stabilized, they do not roll death saves unless submerged, dropped into violent water, or abandoned.

### Burden Rule

The character carrying the unconscious PC:

* may still make checks,
* has +2 DC on checks involving climbing, swimming, squeezing, or resisting the current,
* cannot freely use both hands unless the unconscious PC is secured.

If the carrier fails a physical check by 5 or more, they choose one:

1. **Drop Ground:** The party gains 1 failure, but the unconscious PC remains safe.
2. **Take the Impact:** The carrier takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage and keeps hold of them.
3. **Lose Grip:** The unconscious PC is swept away a short distance. Another PC must immediately make a DC 13 Athletics, Acrobatics, or Medicine check to recover them.

---

## Useful Checks

### Athletics

Carry someone through the flood, climb the Thornshaft, force through debris.

### Acrobatics

Keep footing, leap across broken ledges, avoid being swept away.

### Survival / Nature

Find upward tunnels, read water flow, follow air currents.

### Perception

Hear collapsing stone, spot a safer route, notice a hidden side passage.

### Investigation

Find weak points in blocked exits or old drainage routes.

### Religion

Read the lantern’s reaction to the cleansed water and follow the safest path.

### Medicine

Keep the unconscious PC breathing and above water.

### Spellcasting

Reward creative spell use. Shape Water, Mold Earth, Thunderwave, Guiding Bolt, fire, frost, rope tricks, and similar magic can all count as successes if used cleverly.

---

## Complications

Use one for each failure.

1. A surge slams a PC into dead briar. They take 1d6 piercing damage.
2. The unconscious PC is briefly submerged.
3. A dead root wall collapses and separates the party.
4. Panicked Beastmen claw through the tunnel like drowning animals.
5. A rope slips or snaps during the climb.
6. The lantern flickers and draws attention from something distant.
7. An Ashborn scout sees the clean water and flees.
8. A PC loses a minor item to the flood.

---

## Beastmen Note

The remaining Beastmen are not sentient.

They do not speak, plead, or bargain.

They are mindless animals after the curse takes them.

If encountered here, they are hazards or beasts in panic. They may attack, flee, drown, or claw desperately at the walls.

---

## Escape Outcome

### Full Success

The party emerges from a cracked hillside, collapsed chapel foundation, or old drainage tunnel.

Behind them, clean water continues to pour from Haldrath.

This creates a new landmark:

**The Haldrath Spring.**

### Failure Outcome

The party is swept through a lower rupture in the hillside.

Everyone makes a DC 13 Constitution save.

* Success: 1d6 bludgeoning damage.
* Failure: 2d6 bludgeoning damage and they lose one minor item.

They still escape.

# Act II — Return to Grave’s End

The party returns to Grave’s End exhausted, soaked, and unrested.

They know Kael’ith and Riot were going to Corrin, a rebel contact in the city.

They have not met Corrin yet.

## Finding Corrin

Corrin operates out of a hidden safehouse called **The Third Candle**, beneath an abandoned net-maker’s shop in the Smuggler’s Spine.

Signs:

* three old candle stubs in a cracked window,
* a blue fishing net tied around a rusted hook,
* a chalk mark under the doorframe,
* a child courier watching from an alley,
* a shutter that opens only after the party gives Kael’ith’s name.

## Corrin’s Introduction

Corrin opens the door only wide enough to show one eye and the edge of a knife.

Read or paraphrase:

“You look like you crawled through a grave and lost an argument with a river.”

Their eyes move to the unconscious party member.

“Inside. Quickly. And if you brought trouble with you, bleed on the stone.”

## Corrin’s Priorities

Corrin wants to know:

1. Were they followed?
2. Is the unconscious person dying?
3. Did they come from Haldrath?
4. Did Kael’ith trust them?
5. Do they know Riot is alive?

Corrin does not know what happened beneath Haldrath unless the party tells them.

---

# Meeting Riot and Kael’ith

Riot and Kael’ith are hidden deeper in the safehouse.

## Read-Aloud

The back room is small, low-ceilinged, and lit by a single covered lantern.

Kael’ith stands when you enter. His hand moves first to his weapon, then away from it when he sees you.

Behind him sits Riot.

He looks thinner than he did in the chaos of Karthold. One leg ends where it should not. A crutch leans against the wall beside him. His hands rest on his knees, wrapped but not hidden.

Across one set of knuckles is tattooed the word **Riot**.

Across the other:

**Ruin**.

Kael’ith notices you noticing.

Riot closes his hands.

## The Mood

This is not a reunion of triumph.

Everyone is alive, but nobody is whole.

Riot is frustrated, humiliated, and uncertain. Kael’ith is controlled, protective, and full of guilt. Corrin is trying to keep both of them from making history before sunrise.

---

## Roleplay Beats

Use whichever come naturally.

### Riot’s Self-Doubt

Riot does not think he can lead anymore.

Suggested line:

“Hard to ask people to stand when I’m still learning how.”

### Kael’ith’s Guilt

Kael’ith does not say “I failed you.”

He says things around it.

Suggested line:

“I should have gotten to you sooner.”

Riot answers:

“You did. That was the problem.”

### Old Names

If the party asks about the tattoos:

Riot looks at his fists.

“Riot and Ruin. That was the joke. Then it was a promise. Then it was all anyone called us.”

Kael’ith says nothing.

### Ascension

If Kael’ith’s Ascension comes up:

Riot:

“You left to become one of them.”

Kael’ith:

“I left because we were losing.”

Riot:

“And then we lost anyway.”

### Corrin Interrupts

If the conversation gets too heated:

Corrin:

“You can reopen old wounds after everyone has slept. For now, we keep the living alive.”

# Act III — Hollow Vein Burns

After the long rest, wake the party in the middle of the night.

## Read-Aloud

You wake to bells.

Not one bell. Many.

They ring over one another in a broken, panicked rhythm. For a moment, in the dark of the cellar, it sounds like the whole city is being beaten with iron fists.

Then comes the smell.

Smoke.

Hot pitch.

Burning wood.

Someone upstairs screams, and the ceiling shakes with running feet.

Corrin throws open the cellar door, face lit orange from behind.

“Up,” they say. “Now.”

Another bell cracks somewhere above you, its ringing cut short.

Grave’s End is burning.

## Immediate Information

Corrin has only fragments.

They say:

* “Hollow Vein is burning.”
* “The central street is blocked.”
* “They are asking about Haldrath.”
* “They took Fenrur.”
* “They are not looting. They are searching.”

Then:

“Whatever you did under that keep, someone noticed.”

# Ashborn Awareness

Use this simple track to determine whether the Ashborn identify the party.

Starts at **1**.

## Awareness 0–1: Searching

The Ashborn know the Haldrath cleansers are likely in Grave’s End, but not who they are.

## Awareness 2: Suspicious

The Ashborn have descriptions of outsiders connected to Hollow Vein or Fenrur.

## Awareness 3: Identified

The Ashborn identify at least one party member as connected to Haldrath.

## Awareness 4: Marked

The Prophet has enough information to send future hunters.

---

## Increase Awareness When

* A Hollow Vein patient gives the party away.
* The party openly uses the lantern in front of Ashborn.
* Victor openly uses divine power against them.
* The party announces they were at Haldrath.
* An Ashborn escapes after seeing the party act.
* The Prophet’s projection directly sees them.
* The party attacks the Prophet during his introduction.

## Decrease or Prevent Awareness When

* Hollow Vein patients lie to protect them.
* The party conceals their faces.
* They fight without leaving survivors.
* They use deception to blame another group.
* They rescue witnesses before interrogation breaks them.
* They destroy the Prophet’s projection vessel before it focuses on them.

# Hollow Vein Ward

## Purpose of the Scene

This scene ties the attack to the party’s previous actions.

They healed people here.

Those people may now protect them, betray them, or be used against them.

The Ashborn are not here because they know the party’s names. They are here because Hollow Vein contains witnesses.

## Read-Aloud

Hollow Vein is choked with smoke.

The sick-houses and lean-to clinics glow with reflected fire. Patients stumble into the streets wrapped in blankets. Others are still trapped inside, coughing behind canvas walls and cracked shutters.

Ashborn heretics move through the ward with red ash painted across their faces.

They are not looting.

They are dragging people from beds and asking questions.

“Who healed you?”

“Who came from the fire road?”

“Who spoke to Fenrur?”

“Who came back from Haldrath?”

## Scene Setup

The Ashborn have gathered several patients and caretakers in the street.

One Kindler holds a knife near a patient’s throat.

Another searches the ward for records, names, or signs of unusual healing.

A third is setting fire to the clinic tents.

## Important Witnesses

Use three.

### Mara Vell

Young woman the party healed. Brave but terrified.

She will try to protect them if she can.

### Tovin Brack

Dockhand or laborer the party healed. Weak under threat.

He might reveal that the party came through Hollow Vein.

### Brother Elian

Local caretaker. Protective, exhausted, furious.

He does not know the whole truth, but he knows the party helped.

### Old Senna

Half-blind old woman. Stubborn enough to lie directly to an armed zealot.

She gives a terrible false description if asked.

Example:

“The one you want had red hair, three eyes, and a limp. Now go chase him.”

---

# Hollow Vein Encounter

## Enemies

* 2 Ashborn Kindlers
* 1 Ashborn Interrogator

If the party is especially strong, add one Smoke Hound.

## Objectives

The party should be able to win this scene in several ways.

They can:

* kill or drive off the Ashborn,
* rescue patients,
* prevent witnesses from identifying them,
* capture an interrogator,
* stop the clinic fires.

## Encounter Features

### Burning Clinic

At initiative count 20 each round, fire spreads.

After 3 rounds, trapped patients begin suffering.

A PC can use an action and succeed on a DC 13 Athletics, Medicine, or Survival check to evacuate patients from one burning section.

### Frightened Witnesses

At the end of round 2, Tovin or another weak witness begins to break.

A PC can intervene with:

* Persuasion DC 13,
* Intimidation DC 13,
* Deception DC 14,
* killing/driving away the interrogator,
* creating a distraction,
* using magic creatively.

If no one intervenes, increase Ashborn Awareness by 1.

### Hollow Vein Goodwill

Because the party healed people here, they have **2 Goodwill Holds**.

A Goodwill Hold can be spent when a witness would expose them.

Instead, the witness lies, stays silent, or misdirects the Ashborn.

Use this to reward the party’s earlier compassion.

---

# Hollow Vein Outcomes

## Clean Success

The party saves the patients and prevents identification.

Result:

* Awareness does not increase.
* Hollow Vein protects the party’s secret.
* Brother Elian or Mara tells them Fenrur was taken to the central street.

## Loud Success

The party saves the ward, but the Ashborn see enough.

Result:

* Awareness increases by 1.
* The Ashborn begin calling them “Haldrath-touched” or “wound-thieves.”
* Civilians survive and are grateful.

## Failure

The ward burns badly, or witnesses are taken.

Result:

* Awareness increases by 2.
* One witness is dragged toward the central street.
* The Prophet may use that witness during Fenrur’s execution.

# Clue from Hollow Vein

No matter how the scene ends, the party learns:

Fenrur was taken alive to the central street.

The Ashborn said:

“The old ash will answer before the new fire.”

# Act IV — Central Grave’s End

The party reaches the central portion of Grave’s End.

This is the main public street, market cross, or central switchway plaza.

The Prophet is there.

Fenrur is bound in the street.

The Ashborn heretics have gathered civilians, prisoners, and witnesses.

This is not yet initiative.

# The Central Street

## Read-Aloud

The central street of Grave’s End burns in pieces.

Not all at once. Not wildly. Deliberately.

A wagon burns at one end of the square. A line of oil-fed flame cuts off the lower switchway. Lantern posts have been pulled down and arranged in a circle around the market stones.

At the center of that circle kneels Fenrur.

His hands are bound. His ash-grey robes are torn. Blood runs from his mouth, dark against the soot on his chin.

Around him stand the heretical Ashborn, silent and red-marked.

Then the smoke parts.

A man steps through it.

The fires lean toward him.

## The Prophet’s Appearance

The Prophet should appear physically enough to be sensed and remembered, but not available as a combat encounter.

He is present for the execution and sermon.

He leaves before the main fight begins.

Describe him as:

* ash-streaked,
* black-veined,
* calm,
* fever-bright,
* carrying the smell of Black Blood and smoke,
* not monstrous at first glance, which makes him worse.

## The Prophet’s Purpose Here

He is not here for Riot.

He does not know Riot is alive.

He is here to:

1. punish Fenrur,
2. denounce the old Ashborn ways,
3. ask who cleansed Haldrath,
4. draw the Haldrath cleansers into view,
5. leave Veyr Ashmantle to finish the attack.

---

# Fenrur’s Final Moment

Fenrur should get one moment of dignity.

The Prophet kneels in front of him.

### Fenrur

“Ash remembers. It does not command.”

### Prophet

“Then you have spent your life listening to dust.”

### Fenrur

“You are not ash.”

He coughs blood.

“You are Black Blood wearing it.”

This line matters.

It gives Victor a reason to use Divine Sense.

It also names the truth before Fenrur dies.

## The Murder

The Prophet places one hand on Fenrur’s head.

Black veins move beneath the Prophet’s skin.

Fenrur stiffens.

Ash spills from his mouth.

Then the Prophet whispers:

“When the world is ash, no corruption will remain.”

Fenrur collapses.

Do not make this a fair contest.

This is the Prophet’s introduction.

He murders Fenrur to show what he is.

---

# If the Players Intervene Early

Let them.

Do not shut them down.

But the Prophet still escapes.

## If They Shoot, Charge, or Cast at Him

Their action can matter.

Choose one:

1. They strike him and draw a line of black blood across his cheek.
2. They destroy part of the ash-circle, saving a nearby civilian.
3. They force him to end the execution faster.
4. They interrupt his sermon and prevent Awareness from increasing further.
5. They make him notice them directly.

Then the Prophet leaves.

He steps backward into smoke, black flame, or an ash-collapse.

The main encounter begins immediately as Veyr Ashmantle and the heretics move to intercept.

Do not roll initiative against the Prophet.

Do not give him combat stats.

---

# Victor’s Divine Sense

If Victor uses Divine Sense on the Prophet, give him clear information.

## Read-Aloud

The Prophet is alive.

That is the first horror.

He is not undead. Not a fiend. Not a corpse wearing skin.

He is alive.

But the Black Blood is in him so deeply that it feels less like corruption and more like a second skeleton. It coils through his veins, gathers behind his eyes, and beats in his chest like a second heart.

He is not merely touched by the wound.

He has fed from it.

If Victor uses Divine Sense on the Prophet before he leaves, he learns:

* the Prophet is living,
* he is heavily corrupted by Black Blood,
* the corruption is related to Haldrath,
* the Prophet has willingly consumed or internalized it,
* he is not merely an Ashborn zealot,
* he is a vessel of the thing he claims to burn away.

## Does the Prophet Notice?

Only if useful.

If Awareness is already 3 or higher, the Prophet notices.

He turns toward Victor and says:

“Ah. One of the carrion-priests has learned to smell rot.”

If Awareness is low, Victor can sense him without being noticed.

# Prophet Dialogue

Use only a few lines.

Do not over-explain him.

Possible lines:

* “The wound was silenced.”
* “Black Blood does not become water.”
* “Rot dressed as mercy is still rot.”
* “Who taught the wound to lie?”
* “Bring me the lantern.”
* “Bring me the hand that touched Haldrath.”
* “A world this sick does not need healing. It needs an end.”

## If the Party Is Still Hidden

The Prophet speaks to the crowd.

“The wound-thief hides among you. Very well. Fire is patient.”

## If the Party Is Known

The Prophet looks directly toward them.

“There you are.”

Then, to the Ashborn:

“Do not kill the lantern-bearer. Not yet.”

# Act V — Main Encounter: Veyr Ashmantle

After the Prophet murders Fenrur, he vanishes.

Veyr Ashmantle takes command.

Veyr is the session’s actual combat boss.

## Veyr’s Role

Veyr is the Prophet’s public knife.

He is there to:

* finish the attack,
* mark or identify the Haldrath cleansers,
* burn the central street,
* kill witnesses who resist,
* capture anyone tied to the lantern if possible.

## Read-Aloud

The Prophet vanishes into the smoke.

For half a breath, the square is silent except for the crackling of fire.

Then one of the Ashborn steps forward, face painted in red ash, censer swinging from one hand.

“The Prophet has asked,” he says.

The censer opens. Red sparks spill across the stones.

“Now the fire answers.”

Roll initiative.

---

# Battlefield: The Central Street

Use a rough open square or wide street.

Features:

* Fenrur’s body at the center.
* Three burning ash braziers.
* Civilians trapped near the edges.
* Two burning wagons.
* Fallen lantern posts.
* Smoke patches.
* A raised platform, wagon, or stair where Veyr begins.
* Side alleys for movement or escape.

---

# Combat Objectives

The party can win by defeating Veyr, but the encounter is stronger if they also manage the battlefield.

Objectives:

1. Defeat or drive off Veyr.
2. Destroy or disrupt the three braziers.
3. Save civilians from the central fire.
4. Prevent the Ashborn from identifying them further.
5. Recover Fenrur’s remains or final token.

---

# Braziers of the Cinder Inquest

There are three braziers.

Each one burns with red ash and black sparks.

At the end of each round, if two or more braziers are active, the fire worsens.

After 4 rounds with two or more braziers active, the ritual completes.

## If the Ritual Completes

Choose one:

* Awareness increases by 1.
* Veyr learns the face of the lantern-bearer.
* The central street suffers heavy damage.
* A civilian witness is branded with ash and becomes a future liability.
* A Smoke Hound is empowered and gains 10 temporary HP.

## Stopping a Brazier

A brazier can be stopped by:

* dealing 12 damage to it,
* Religion DC 14,
* Arcana DC 14,
* dumping water or sand onto it,
* using the Haldrath water,
* using Zeraph’s lantern creatively,
* using appropriate magic.

---

# Civilians in the Square

At initiative count 20 each round, one group of civilians is endangered by spreading fire, collapsing wood, or Ashborn violence.

A PC can use an action to save them with:

* Athletics DC 13,
* Persuasion DC 13,
* Intimidation DC 13,
* Medicine DC 13,
* Survival DC 13,
* or relevant magic.

If ignored for two rounds, civilians die or are captured.

Saving civilians may make the party visible.

If the party saves civilians dramatically in full view, increase Awareness by 1 unless they are disguised or careful.

# Veyr Ashmantle

Use a modified Cult Fanatic.

## Stats

* AC 13
* HP 38
* Speed 30 ft.
* Spell Save DC 12
* Spell Attack +4

## Attacks

### Ashen Flame

Ranged spell attack, +4 to hit, range 60 ft.

Hit: 1d8 fire damage.

### Censer Strike

Melee weapon attack, +4 to hit.

Hit: 1d6+2 bludgeoning plus 1d4 fire damage.

## Spells

Use only what is useful.

* Burning Hands, 1/day
* Hold Person, 1/day
* Command, 2/day
* Sacred Flame reflavored as falling ash

Avoid using too much control if the party is already under pressure.

## Special Ability: Voice of the Pyre

Once per round, Veyr chooses one visible flame within 60 feet.

It surges.

One creature within 5 feet of that flame must make a DC 13 Dexterity save.

Failure: 2d6 fire damage.

Success: half damage.

## Reaction: Ashen Rebuke

When Veyr is hit by a melee attack, he may force the attacker to make a DC 12 Constitution save.

# Fenrur’s Final Clue

After the fight, the party may recover something from Fenrur.

Choose one.

## Fenrur’s Ash Bowl

A small black bowl used in older Ashborn rites.

Inside is grey ash untouched by the Prophet’s red ash.

Fenrur had scratched a symbol into the underside: a circle broken by a single falling line.

Fenrur told them before, or Corrin/Fenrur’s notes can reveal:

Old Ashborn used this symbol to mean:

**Remember, but do not become.**

## Fenrur’s Last Breath

If a PC reaches him immediately after the Prophet leaves, Fenrur is not quite dead.

He grabs their wrist and says:

“He drinks it.”

Then:

“He calls it fire.”

Then he dies.

This points directly to the Prophet consuming Black Blood.

## Fenrur’s Burn Mark

The Prophet’s handprint remains burned into Fenrur’s face or chest.

Victor’s Divine Sense or a Religion check reveals that the mark is not simple fire.

It is Black Blood corruption forced into ash.

# Final Scene Options

Choose one based on the session.

## Ending A — Hidden Victory

Dawn comes grey and sour.

The fires are out, though smoke still clings to the central street. Fenrur’s body has been wrapped in a burned curtain. Hollow Vein still stands.

Corrin watches the street through a cracked shutter.

“They came looking for a miracle,” they say. “They left without a name.”

Behind them, Riot says nothing.

Kael’ith only checks the exits again.

## Ending B — Revealed Victory

The last brazier dies.

The central street is saved, but not silent. Somewhere in the smoke, an Ashborn voice cries out:

“The lantern-bearer!”

Another answers:

“The wound-thief!”

The names spread like sparks.

Corrin turns toward the party.

“Tell me they are guessing.”

Kael’ith is already moving toward Riot.

“They are not.”

## Ending C — Hollow Vein Falls

By dawn, Hollow Vein is blackened.

The ward still stands in pieces, but the sick beds are empty, dragged into the street or carried away. The people the party healed look at them with gratitude, fear, and guilt.

Brother Elian sits on the curb with ash in his hair.

“They kept asking about you,” he says.

Then, quieter:

“I don’t know who broke first.”

## Ending D — The Prophet’s Fear

Far beyond Grave’s End, where the burned edge of Palor meets the black hills, the Prophet kneels in ash.

Before him sits a shallow bowl of water taken from Haldrath.

He sprinkles red ash into it.

The ash floats.

It does not stain.

It pales, curls inward, and sinks.

For the first time, the Prophet does not look angry.

He looks afraid.

# Running Notes

## Keep the Session Focused

Do not offer the party five different burning districts.

The intended path is:

1. Escape Haldrath.
2. Find Corrin.
3. Rest.
4. Hollow Vein.
5. Central Street.
6. Fenrur’s murder.
7. Veyr encounter.
8. Fallout.

This keeps the attack clear and story-driven.

## Keep Riot Hidden

Riot should not publicly reveal himself unless the players force it.

His emotional role is private.

He wants to act.

Kael’ith prevents it.

That tension is the seed for the later sacrifice.

## Do Not Reveal the Rebel Takeover Plan

The party should not learn that the Prophet may want to manipulate rebel forces.

This session shows only:

* he uses fear well,
* he understands public violence,
* he targets symbols,
* his doctrine appeals to grief and rage,
* he creates the exact conditions that make desperate people radical.

The larger plan can emerge later.

## Let the Party Choose Visibility

This is the session’s main agency.

They can save people quietly.

Or they can stand openly in the fire.

Both are valid.

Both have consequences.

## The Prophet Is Not a Boss Fight

He appears.

He murders Fenrur.

He speaks.

He may be sensed by Victor.

He leaves.

The fight is against Veyr and the heretical Ashborn.

If players attack the Prophet, let the action matter, but do not turn it into combat. They may wound him, interrupt him, save a bystander, or force him to notice them.

Then he vanishes.

The important result is not damage.

The important result is that the Prophet and the party become aware of one another.
