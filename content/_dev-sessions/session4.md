## Whispers on the Pier
**Party Level:** 2<br>
**Session Length:** ~3–4 hours<br>
**Primary Threat:** Exposure to Harrivale Imperials<br>
**Combat:** Possible, strongly discouraged<br>

## The Pale Wench - the Crew

**The Pale Wench**
Smuggler-cutter, shallow draft, built for speed and discretion

### Crew Size

**14 total**, including the captain. Lean crew, experienced, reliant on trust and routine rather than numbers.
All crew are **Tieflings**.


### Command & Officers

**Captain — Veylan Corrix**
Measured, secretive, not a shouter. Keeps authority by knowing when *not* to act.

**First Mate — Serah “Temperance” Ilvar**
Runs watches, discipline, and day-to-day order. Her virtue name is deliberate and taken seriously.

**Quartermaster — Joren Pell**
Manages pay, stores, and disputes. Practical to the point of cold. No virtue name, by choice.

**Bosun — Grit Hask**
Hull, rigging, deck discipline. Knows every sound the ship makes. Blunt, physically imposing, and deeply protective of the Pale Wench. His surname is shared with dockside relatives—nothing symbolic about it.

### Navigation & Signals

**Navigator — Bash Vol**
Keeps charts, tides, and hidden routes. Quiet, observant, keeps coded notes.

**Signal Lead / Lookout — Nyxen “Vigil” Rook**
Handles lantern codes, flags, and horn signals. The virtue name started as sarcasm and stuck.

### Crew & Specialists

**Ship’s Musician / Morale — Lysa “Revel” Thorne**
Singer and fiddler. Leads work songs, tavern shanties, and keeps spirits up when runs go bad. Highly personable, emotionally intelligent, and often the social glue of the crew. Hears everything, comments on little.

**Cook — Dama Rheel**
Hard-nosed, efficient, knows how to stretch rations. Knows everyone’s habits and grudges.

### Deckhands (6)

* **Tovin Grael** — Veteran sailor, dependable, taciturn
* **Lessa “Mercy” Dael** — Knows basic stitching and salves; virtue name is half-ironic
* **Irik Fenn** — Young, agile, climbs like a spider
* **Brone Hask** — Grit’s cousin; heavy labor, bad knee
* **Maelin Korr** — Net-mender, cook’s mate, sharp tongue
* **Pyr “Fortune” Vesh** — Chronic gambler, insists the name will “turn someday”

### Notes for Play

* Virtue names are **common but not universal**
* Some are inherited, others earned, others mockery
* Grit is a key loyalty anchor for the ship
* Lysa is the easiest social entry point for outsiders
* Serah and Joren quietly balance each other—order vs survival

This version keeps Grit and Lysa in their established roles while grounding the rest of the crew as people, not archetypes.

## Session Premise (DM-Facing)

Captain **Veylan Mirth** has tasked the party with recovering a missing lockbox. He believes it was stolen by someone within his crew. The party’s motivation is simple: **recover the box and earn passage off Karthold**.

The truth is messier:

* The lockbox was stolen by **outside smugglers**
* Grit attempted (and failed) to interfere
* Nobody opened the box/understands what’s inside
* The Imperials are about to arrive for unrelated—but converging—reasons

## Canon Facts

* The lockbox has **never been opened**
* The box is magically sealed; the key is believed lost
* Veylan knows what is inside only from the **journal of the former owner**
* Grit tried to open the box and failed
* Smugglers stole the entire lockbox intact
* Session begins at **midday**, immediately after Session Three
* The party are escaped prisoners from the Oubliette
* Harrivale Imperials can decisively end the party’s escape

## Act I — The Docks & The Scuttle’s Lantern

### Purpose of the Act

* Establish atmosphere
* Point suspicion toward Grit
* Introduce the idea that **outsiders were involved**

### At the Tavern: The Scuttle’s Lantern

The tavern is loud, but the laughter feels forced.

#### **Automatic Observations**

* Several crew members drinking early
* Arguments that stop when PCs get close
* Grit is not present

**Persuasion (DC 11)**
* Crew is on edge because of outsiders
* Veylan has been guarding something valuable

**Insight (DC 12)**
* People are worried about consequences, not punishment
* They seem to look to certain people about the room

**Insight (DC 14)**
* The crew looks to the ship's officers in lieu of Grit

**Intimidation (DC 12)**
* Someone heard smugglers were asking about Veylan’s warehouse days ago

Failure here increases gossip about the party being involved.

### Musician of the *Pale Wench*

**Name:** Lysa<br>
**Role:** Deckhand / informal morale officer<br>
**Vibe:** Bard-adjacent without being a spellcaster. Warm, sharp-eyed, remembers names.<br>

Lysa is the one leading the song spilling out of the Scuttle’s Lantern at midday. She’s got a weathered fiddle and a voice that carries—salt-rough but confident. She’s personable, easy to talk to, and clearly trusted by Veylan’s crew. She knows how to read a room and steer a mood, often acting as the social glue of the *Pale Wench*.

She doesn’t know anything concrete about the lockbox, but:

* She’s aware tensions on the crew have been high since it went missing
* She knows Grit and Ashkel both by name and history
* If pressed gently, she’ll mention Grit has been “keeping low” since the warehouse scare

She can serve as:

* A soft entry point into crew dynamics
* A way to reinforce that Veylan’s crew isn’t unified or stupid
* A contrast to the harder, more paranoid smugglers elsewhere

### On the Docks

**Perception (DC 11)**
* Drag marks from Veylan’s warehouse
* Recently disturbed cargo

**Key Shift**
* This doesn’t look like an inside job anymore.

### What Actually Happened (DM Truth)

* Grit learned smugglers planned to raid the warehouse
* He tried to open the lockbox first to remove its contents
* He failed
* The smugglers arrived later and stole the box intact

### Evidence in the Warehouse

**Automatic**

* Lockbox missing
* Crate broken open where it once sat
* Scorch marks surrounding the crate

**Investigation (DC 11)**
* Ashen prints left indicating the person left after the incident
* Multiple footprints separate to the ashen footprints surround drag marks on the ground, going the other way

**Ledger**
* Grit signed off on an unmarked box matching the one that was broken being brought to the warehouse.
* No mention of the box's contents implies not many knew of the crate's contents.

Implication: Smugglers took the box whole, but Grit is still suspicious.

## Act IV — Boatswain Grit (Revised)

### Where to Find Grit — Social Encounter Location

**Location:** The Saltworks Ruin, just off the southern docks

Grit is not at the Scuttle’s Lantern. He’s sequestered himself to the ship, ordering a few of the crew up to fasten the riggings. 

**Scene Tone:** Paranoid, defensive, but not hostile.
Grit is watching the road, armed, expecting either smugglers or Imperials. He hasn’t slept much.

---

### Grit, Boatswain
**Base Stat Block:** Bandit (MM p.343)
**Modifications**
* HP: 16
* STR-based attacks at disadvantage (burned hands)
* +2 Deception
* No multiattack

**Design Note:**
Grit is dangerous because of what he knows and who he can point the party toward, not because of combat capability.

### Grit’s Condition
* Hands wrapped in dirty cloth, blistered and cracked
* Irritable, paranoid, and defensive

He is not trying to hide what happened — he is trying to control *who knows what*.

### Initial Attitude
**Hostile, accusatory.**

Grit assumes:
* Veylan is lying to the party
* The party are either patsies or scouts
* This situation is already spiraling

### Social Pressure & Skill Checks

**Insight (DC 10)**
Grit isn’t angry about being blamed — he’s angry that *nobody listened to him*.

**Deception (DC 11)**
Claims his hands were injured unloading cargo (false).

**Persuasion (DC 11)**
He grudgingly admits smugglers were sniffing around the warehouse *before* the theft.

**Intimidation (DC 12)**
He snaps and tells the party they’re already too late.

### What Grit Admits (Sequential, Not All at Once)

1. He was tipped off that smugglers were planning to hit Veylan’s warehouse
2. He tried to open the lockbox first, to remove whatever was inside
3. He failed — badly — and panicked
4. The smugglers arrived later and took the entire box intact
5. He knows **who arranged the move**

This is the pivot point.

### Pointing to Ashkel

**Connection to Ashkel:**
Grit explicitly points the party toward Ashkel.

* He admits Ashkel was the only one he trusted with the information
* He insists Ashkel didn’t mean for it to go this far
* He believes Ashkel knows *where* the Blackfish stashed the box, even if he didn’t steal it

Grit does **not** volunteer Ashkel’s name immediately.

He will only do so if:
* The party shows warehouse evidence
* They convince him they aren’t working for Veylan blindly
* Or they promise not to involve the Imperials (even if they’re lying)

When he finally relents:
* “You want answers? Go talk to **Ashkel Var**.
* Calls himself *Redwake*.
* Runner liaison. Knows how things disappear.”

He makes it clear:
* Ashkel didn’t steal the box personally
* Ashkel knows *where it went*
* Ashkel is already scared

### Grit’s Stakes
If Ashkel talks, things might stay quiet.
If the party bungles this, Grit is prepared to tip off Harrivale — not out of spite, but because he believes that’s the only way to end this cleanly.

## Act V — Ashkel “Redwake” Var (Revised)

### Ashkel “Redwake” Var
**Base Stat Block:** Commoner (MM p.345)
**Modifications**
* Advantage on Deception checks related to smuggling
* Proficient in Insight and Stealth

**Design Note:**
Ashkel is socially agile and well-informed, not powerful. He is as important as Grit, but from the opposite side of the fence.

### Where to Find Him
**Location:**
A narrow, weather-beaten pier jutting out from the Blacktide docks, half used by fishing skiffs and half by smugglers who don’t want eyes on them. Nets hang from posts, crates are stacked unevenly, and the water below is dark and slow-moving.

Ashkel is **openly drinking** here in the middle of the day with a handful of Blackfish smugglers. A shared bottle is being passed around, laughter a little too loud, tension barely disguised as bravado. 

Ashkel spots the party as they approach. He straightens, wipes his mouth, and his tone shifts immediately into guarded humor.

### Setup & Tone

Ashkel is:

* Drunk enough to be careless
* Sober enough to be afraid
* Trying to look untouchable

The Blackfish around him are relaxed but alert. They don’t draw weapons immediately, but they’re positioned to box the pier in if things go sideways.

### Escalation Trigger

If the party:
* Accuses Ashkel openly
* Threatens him
* Mentions Imperials
* Acts aggressive or condescending

Ashkel gives a casual signal—tipping the bottle, a hand on a pier post, a laugh that cuts short.

**Ashkel (to the smugglers):**
“Think these outsiders need reminding whose pier this is.”

At that point, the Blackfish move to “teach a lesson.”

### Blackfish Smugglers — Pier Fight

**Enemies:**
* **3 Blackfish Smugglers** (Bandit stat blocks)
 
**Environment:**
* Narrow pier (forced positioning, risk of falling into water)
* Light cover from stacked crates and nets
* Difficult terrain near the pier edge if someone goes overboard

**Tactics:**
* Focus on shoving, grappling, and knocking targets into the water
* One smuggler blocks retreat toward shore
* If two smugglers drop, the rest hesitate

**Ashkel’s Role:**
* He does not fight unless attacked directly
* He shouts for it to stop if someone is seriously wounded
* If things turn deadly, he tries to flee into the docks


### Aftermath Notes (Unchanged Stakes)

**If Ashkel Survives:**

* Social pressure or victory forces him to talk
* He gives up the orehouse location reluctantly
* He stresses he never touched or opened the lockbox

**If Ashkel Dies:**

* The note to **Viscera** is found on him as before
* Confirms the ore storehouse location and sigil
* Reinforces that Ashkel was a link, not the architect

This keeps Ashkel reckless rather than cunning, reinforces his fear-driven behavior, and makes the confrontation feel like a public mistake rather than a planned ambush.

### Initial Attitude
**Desperate, de-escalating.**

Ashkel assumes:
* Grit already talked
* The party might be trying to pin this on him
* The Imperials are only hours or days away

### Social Encounter Structure

**Insight (DC 11)**
Ashkel is afraid of being blamed, not of the party.

**Persuasion (DC 11)**
He admits he let slip the location while he was drunk with some Blackfish mates- the Quartermaster Viscera decided to pull the job.

**Deception (DC 12)**
Ashkel tries to downplay his role — he arranged logistics, not the theft.

**Intimidation (DC 12)**
He cracks and confirms the location.

### Ashkel’s Version of Events

Ashkel provides crucial context Grit does not:
* Grit told Ashkel about the lockbox in confidence
* Ashkel tipped off others in his crew, a new quartermaster named Viscera decided to act on it unbeknownst to Ashkel
* A new upstart quartermaster for the Blackfish smugglers planned the job- someone named Viscera.
* Ashkel never touched it, never opened it
* He confirms the location of where its being held by the 

### The Orehouse Reveal

Ashkel gives the location plainly once pressed:
* An **old ore storehouse**, inland
* Half-collapsed by a landslide years ago
* Used when goods need to vanish for a few days
* The smugglers planned to come back later

He refuses to go with the party.

### Ashkel’s Stakes

* If the box disappears quietly, Ashkel survives
* If Imperials get involved, Ashkel runs or sells someone out
* He considers Grit a liability, but not an enemy

This frames Grit and Ashkel as two sides of the same bad decision.
