# Session 16 — The Wound Answers

## Current Continuity

The party has escaped Haldrath after cleansing the Black Blood font. They camped overnight in the forest and advanced to **level 4**. They chose to hunt the Prophet rather than immediately return to Grave’s End.

Their trail led them back to Grave’s End.

When they arrived, the city was already under attack by heretical Ashborn druids. They entered the streets and found druids attacking civilians and setting fires. Some attackers mentioned searching for someone with a lantern: Zeraph.

The party has not yet met up with Corrin, Kael’ith, or Riot. They do not know where those three are.

---

# Session Purpose

This session should make the players feel that Grave’s End is endangered, but not doomed.

The Prophet’s attack should hurt the city, kill people, and introduce his threat directly. But the party should be able to save enough of Grave’s End that it remains a place of safety, recovery, and planning afterward.

The intended emotional arc:

1. The party sees that the Prophet’s followers are hunting Zeraph.
2. The party helps stabilize civilians and fire lines.
3. The party follows the pressure toward the center of town.
4. The Prophet publicly murders Fenrur and names the Haldrath miracle as his target.
5. The party breaks the ritual fire blocking the route to the docks.
6. Grave’s End survives, damaged but still livable.
7. Corrin finds the party after the battle and brings them toward the hidden rebel thread.

---

# Session Time Budget

Target runtime: **3 hours**

* Opening aftermath and information: 10–15 minutes
* Burning ward skill challenge: 25–35 minutes
* Burning descent: 10–15 minutes
* Prophet and Fenrur scene: 15–20 minutes
* Main encounter: 75–95 minutes
* Docks and city survives: 10–15 minutes
* Corrin / safehouse hook: 15–25 minutes

If time runs short, end with Corrin finding the party and revealing that Kael’ith and Riot are alive and hidden nearby.

# Core Tracking Mechanics

## City Damage

Track **City Damage** from 0 to 6.

Start at **2**.

Increase City Damage by 1 when:

* the party fails a major fire/rescue challenge,
* the ritual braziers remain active too long,
* civilians are ignored in immediate danger,
* the party spends too long pursuing a side objective,
* the central barricade remains uncleared after the main fight,
* enemies deliberately ignite a key structure.

Decrease City Damage by 1 when:

* the party gets civilians moving to the docks,
* the party suppresses or redirects a major fire,
* the party disables ritual braziers quickly,
* the party clears the route to the docks,
* the party saves key infrastructure,
* the party uses creative magic or resources to protect civilians.

### City Damage Results

**0–2: Scarred but stable.**
Grave’s End loses people and buildings, but most districts remain livable. Hollow Vein survives. The docks function. Corrin’s network remains intact.

**3–4: Badly damaged.**
Several streets and buildings are lost. Hollow Vein is partially ruined. Families shelter near the docks. Grave’s End remains usable, but recovery becomes a visible need.

**5: Wounded city.**
A major section burns. The lower districts become temporary shelters. Corrin’s network is strained. The party may need to spend part of next session helping recovery or deciding what can be saved.

**6: Near-disaster.**
Grave’s End survives only barely. Avoid this unless the party ignores most major objectives or suffers repeated catastrophic failures.

The intended ending is **2–4**.

---

## Lantern Attention

The attackers are looking for the lantern-bearer.

Track **Lantern Attention** from 0 to 3.
Start at **1**, because some druids have already mentioned the lantern.

Increase Lantern Attention by 1 when:

* Zeraph uses the lantern openly in front of Ashborn enemies,
* a captured or fleeing enemy sees Zeraph clearly and escapes,
* the party loudly declares their involvement in Haldrath,
* Zeraph uses the lantern against a ritual brazier,
* civilians loudly identify the party as the ones who helped Hollow Vein.

Decrease Lantern Attention by 1 when:

* the party kills or captures all witnesses in a small enemy group,
* the party fights from smoke, cover, or confusion,
* another PC deliberately draws attention away from Zeraph,
* civilians intentionally misdirect attackers because of Hollow Vein goodwill.

### Lantern Attention Results

**0–1: Unconfirmed.**
The Prophet knows the lantern-bearer is connected to Grave’s End but leaves without a clear identity.

**2: Suspected.**
The Prophet or Veyr strongly suspects Zeraph or the party. Future Ashborn hunters can identify them by description.

**3: Confirmed.**
The Prophet knows the lantern-bearer was present and receives enough details to pursue Zeraph directly later.

This should affect future pressure, not derail this session.

## Hollow Vein Goodwill

Because the party previously helped Hollow Vein, they have **2 Goodwill Holds** during this session.

Spend 1 Goodwill Hold to cause one:

* Civilians follow a party instruction without a check.
* A patient warns the party of danger.
* A local throws sand, blankets, or water onto a fire.
* A civilian points out a safer route.
* A group clears space for the party to move.
* A witness refuses to identify Zeraph clearly.
* A caretaker helps move wounded civilians toward the docks.

# Scene 1 — After the First Street Fight

## Scene Position

This picks up immediately after the party’s first clash with heretical Ashborn in Grave’s End.

The party is in the burning streets. Civilians are nearby. Smoke is increasing. Some attackers may be dead, fleeing, or captured.

## Goals

This scene should quickly establish:

* the attack is organized,
* the attackers are searching for the lantern-bearer,
* the fires are being guided toward the center of town,
* civilians are moving toward the docks,
* the center street is blocked,
* Fenrur has been taken or is missing.

## What the Party Can Learn

### From Civilians

Civilians are panicked, injured, and trying to reach the docks. They know the docks are the safest place because of the open air, water, stone quays, and bucket lines.

They may know:

* Hollow Vein is burning.
* The attackers came from the upper smoke and forest edge.
* The attackers were asking about someone with a lantern.
* Some attackers were asking who returned from the old human ruins.
* Fenrur was taken toward the center of town.
* The central route to the docks is blocked by unnatural fire.

### From Dead or Captured Ashborn

A quick search reveals:

* red ash smeared on their hands and foreheads,
* scorched prayer strips,
* small vials of diluted Black Blood,
* fire-starting tools,
* black briar thorns used as ritual pins,
* rough markings of Hollow Vein and the center street,
* signs that the attack was meant to draw someone out, not destroy the whole city.

### Investigation or Medicine: Diluted Black Blood Vials

A PC who examines the vials can learn the following.

**DC 12 Medicine or Nature:**
The vials are not ordinary poison. They contain diluted Black Blood mixed with ash, alcohol, and bitter herbal stabilizers.

**DC 15 Medicine:**
The mixture could suppress pain and shock. Someone dosed with it might fight through wounds they should feel.

**DC 18 Medicine:**
The mixture might stabilize the dying or stop bleeding, but it does not heal cleanly. It forces tissue to close in a blackened, rigid way.

**DC 20+ Medicine:**
Dilution does not make Black Blood safe. It makes it slow. Repeated use could cause numbness, black veins, aggression, disturbed sleep, altered hunger, hardened tissue, and early bestial changes.

**DC 25 Medicine:**
The full conclusion: diluted Black Blood is corrupted medicine. It can keep a body alive by teaching it to survive incorrectly. It preserves, numbs, seals, and adapts flesh, but every use pushes the body further from its original nature.

## Motives in the Scene

### Civilians

Desire: Survive, find family, reach water, avoid smoke.
Fear: Being trapped inside burning streets.
Detest: Being used as bait or ignored by armed people.
Useful behavior: They can give quick information, but they cannot calmly plan.

### Heretical Ashborn

Desire: Spread controlled fire, flush out the lantern-bearer, prove the Prophet’s doctrine.
Fear: The lantern, clean water from Haldrath, loss of ritual control.
Detest: Old Ashborn restraint, mercy without burning, people calling their fire murder.
Useful behavior: They fight violently and ask questions while attacking, but they do not take hostages.

# Scene 2 — Burning Ward Skill Challenge

## Situation

The party pushes deeper into the burning ward. Civilians are trapped by fire, smoke, falling debris, and panic. The route to the docks is not yet open, but people must be moved toward it before the smoke overtakes the district.

This is not a scene about evacuating the city. It is about moving people to the safest place inside the city: the docks.

## Challenge Goal

Get civilians moving from Hollow Vein toward the docks.

## Difficulty

**4 successes before 3 failures.**
Default DC: **13**
Use DC 15 for risky, improvised, or large-scale actions.

Each PC can contribute once freely. A PC can contribute again if they spend a resource, take a risk, or describe a meaningfully different approach.

## Success

On 4 successes:

* civilians begin moving in an organized flow toward the central street,
* reduce City Damage by 1,
* the party gains momentum toward the main encounter,
* the first round of the main encounter may begin with civilians slightly more controlled.

## Failure

On 3 failures:

* the party still gets civilians moving,
* increase City Damage by 1,
* each PC who participated in the challenge chooses one consequence:

  * take 1d6 fire or poison damage,
  * spend 1 Hit Die without healing to represent strain,
  * gain disadvantage on their next ability check during the burning descent,
  * start the next scene separated from one ally by smoke or debris.

Failure does not stop the story. The ward moves, but it costs more.

---

## Relevant Skill Uses

### Athletics

Move fallen beams, drag civilians from smoke, force open swollen doors, carry wounded people.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The PC succeeds but strains themselves. They take 1d6 bludgeoning damage or have their speed reduced by 10 feet until the end of the next scene.

### Acrobatics

Cross unstable walkways, move through collapsing spaces, reach civilians through narrow gaps.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The PC makes it through but falls hard or inhales smoke. They take 1d6 damage or begin the next scene prone/behind the group.

### Medicine

Stabilize smoke victims, triage who must move first, prevent panic around the sick.

**Success:** Gain 1 success. One injured civilian group can move safely.
**Failure complication:** The PC saves them, but it takes too long. Mark a delay. Two delays become +1 City Damage.

### Persuasion

Calm civilians and convince them to move toward the docks.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The civilians move, but slowly. The next check related to crowd movement is made at disadvantage.

### Intimidation

Cut through panic with command presence.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** People move, but fear spreads. Increase Lantern Attention by 1 if the party is visibly forceful and memorable.

### Performance

Use voice, rhythm, song, or presence to coordinate movement through chaos.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The PC draws attention. A distant Ashborn spots or hears them; increase Lantern Attention by 1 unless another PC intervenes.

### Survival

Read fire movement and choose the safest path.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The route works, but cuts through heavier smoke. Each PC must make a DC 12 Constitution save or take 1d6 poison damage.

### Nature

Understand smoke, wind, burning wood, and firebreaks.

**Success:** Gain 1 success. Reduce the next fire hazard by one step.
**Failure complication:** The PC predicts correctly, but too late. A small fire spreads behind the group, cutting off retreat.

### Perception

Spot trapped civilians, unstable roofs, hidden side routes, or approaching attackers.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The PC spots the danger just in time but must choose: protect civilians or protect the party. If civilians are protected, one PC takes 1d6 damage. If the party is protected, mark a delay.

### Religion

Identify heretical Ashborn fire marks and avoid the worst ritual flames.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The PC identifies the mark, but contact with red ash causes a pulse of dread. They have disadvantage on their next Wisdom saving throw before the end of the session.

### Arcana

Disrupt minor ritual flame or identify which fires are mundane versus unnatural.

**Success:** Gain 1 success.
**Failure complication:** The ritual resists. The PC takes 1d4 fire damage and the next brazier or ritual object they interact with has +2 DC.

### Spellcasting

Creative spell use should usually count as a success if it meaningfully solves a problem.

Examples:

* Create or destroy water suppresses a fire.
* Gust clears smoke.
* Healing Word or Cure Wounds stabilizes a key civilian.
* Light or the lantern guides people.
* Thorn Whip, Mage Hand, or similar magic clears debris or pulls someone free.

**Failure complication:** If the spell is underpowered for the task, it still helps, but the caster draws attention, spends extra time, or creates a partial hazard.

---

## Complication Table

Use one on each failed check, or choose what fits.

1. A roof beam falls behind the party, cutting off the easiest retreat.
2. A civilian group panics and surges into a smoke bank.
3. A sick-house door swells shut with people still inside.
4. A hidden cinder flask ignites stacked cloth and oil.
5. A child or elder falls behind and must be retrieved.
6. The smoke thickens; all sight-based checks are made at disadvantage until someone clears the air.
7. A wounded civilian mistakes the party for attackers and causes panic.
8. A dying Ashborn crawls toward a fire source to reignite it.
9. A building collapse separates one PC from the group until they spend movement or an action rejoining.
10. Someone recognizes Zeraph’s lantern if it is visible; increase Lantern Attention by 1.

# Scene 3 — Burning Descent

## Situation

The party leads or follows civilians downhill through switchback streets and smoke-heavy alleys. The docks are visible below in flashes: lanterns, wet stone, boats, bucket lines, and people gathering near the water.

The center street is between the ward and the docks.

The party should realize the main fire source is ahead.

## Challenge Goal

Reach the central street before the ritual fire causes more severe damage.

## Difficulty

**3 successes before 2 failures.**
Default DC: **13**

## Success

The party reaches the central street in good position.

Choose one benefit:

* reduce City Damage by 1,
* the party gains advantage on initiative for the main encounter,
* civilians begin the main encounter slightly farther from the fire,
* one PC may start the encounter near a useful piece of cover or a brazier.

## Failure

The party still reaches the central street, but with a cost.

Choose one complication:

* increase City Damage by 1,
* one civilian group begins the main encounter in immediate danger,
* the party starts split between two nearby street entrances,
* each PC must make a DC 12 Constitution save or take 1d6 poison damage from smoke,
* the ritual braziers begin with one additional surge already charged.

---

## Relevant Skills

### Survival or Nature

Find the safest path through smoke and fire.

Failure complication: The route works, but the party arrives through thicker smoke. Disadvantage on the first Perception check or ranged attack in the next scene.

### Athletics

Move debris, carry wounded, force through a blocked street.

Failure complication: The PC succeeds but takes 1 level of temporary strain. Until the end of the next encounter, the first time they Dash or Shove, they take 1d4 damage from strain and smoke.

### Perception

Spot a safer route, unstable stair, or ambush point.

Failure complication: The danger is noticed late. One PC must make a DC 13 Dexterity save or take 1d6 bludgeoning damage.

### Persuasion / Intimidation / Performance

Keep the crowd moving.

Failure complication: The crowd moves, but loudly. Increase Lantern Attention by 1 if the party is visible and commanding.

### Medicine

Keep the weakest civilians from collapsing.

Failure complication: The PC saves them, but spends precious time. Mark one delay; two delays increase City Damage by 1.

### Religion

Track the direction of the ritual fire.

Failure complication: The PC follows the signs but brushes against red ash. They feel the Prophet’s doctrine as a hostile presence and have disadvantage on their next check to interact with a brazier.

# Scene 4 — Central Street and Fenrur

## Situation

The central street is blocked by ritual fire. Civilians cannot reach the docks. Heretical Ashborn have turned the street into a choke point with fallen lantern posts, burning carts, overturned stalls, and three red-burning braziers.

Fenrur is bound at the center.

The Prophet appears here.

## Scene Purpose

This scene should:

* show the Prophet directly,
* kill Fenrur,
* clarify that the attack is about Haldrath and the lantern,
* demonstrate the Prophet’s corruption,
* set up the main encounter.

## Prophet Motives

Desire: Find the one who cleansed Haldrath and prove that cleansing is a false mercy.
Fear: That Black Blood can actually be healed, making his doctrine unnecessary and cruel.
Detests: Old Ashborn restraint, mourning without action, mercy that preserves life, light that cleanses rather than burns.
Relationship to Fenrur: Fenrur represents the older Ashborn way. The Prophet kills him to publicly declare that restraint is dead.
Relationship to Zeraph: Zeraph is not personally known to him yet, but the lantern-bearer is the central object of his hunt.
Relationship to Seethe: Seethe represents a possible contradiction: an Ashborn who may still believe in controlled fire and penance.
Relationship to Victor: Victor can sense the Black Blood in him, which may make the Prophet briefly notice him.
Relationship to the city: Grave’s End is not his true target. It is pressure applied to reveal the target.

## Fenrur Motives

Desire: Protect the older Ashborn tradition and keep fire bound to memory, penance, and restoration.
Fear: That the Ashborn will be remembered only through the Prophet’s cruelty.
Detests: Fire used as command, spectacle, or punishment without restoration.
Role in scene: He dies as the living contrast to the Prophet.

## Interrupting the Prophet

Do not make the Prophet a fightable enemy in this session.

If the party acts against him before he vanishes, let the action matter.

Possible outcomes:

* A successful attack draws a thin line of black blood.
* A spell disrupts one brazier before combat begins.
* A PC saves a nearby civilian from ritual flame.
* A successful Religion, Arcana, or Nature check reveals how wrong the ritual fire is.
* Victor’s Divine Sense reveals the Black Blood inside the Prophet.
* Seethe recognizes the Prophet’s fire as a violation of older Ashborn tenets.
* Zeraph’s lantern flares, confirming that the Prophet notices the impossible light.

After one meaningful intervention, the Prophet vanishes into smoke, ash, or black flame. His followers remain.

## Victor’s Divine Sense Result

The Prophet is alive.

Not undead. Not fiend. Not corpse.

Alive.

But Black Blood runs through him so deeply that it feels like a second skeleton. It coils through his veins, gathers behind his eyes, and beats in his chest like another heart.

He is not merely touched by corruption.

He has fed from it.

Mechanical effect: Victor gains advantage on one future Insight, Religion, or attack roll involving the Prophet or his direct corruption.

## Seethe’s Ashborn Recognition

With no roll required, Seethe can tell the Prophet’s rites are Ashborn-derived but wrong.

With a DC 13 Religion or Nature check, Seethe knows:

* red ash is being used where grey ash should be,
* the ritual is commanding flame rather than tending it,
* the fire is meant to spread fear, not cleanse a wound,
* Fenrur was killed as a rejection of old Ashborn restraint.

Mechanical effect: On success, Seethe has advantage on one check to disable a brazier or counter a heretical Ashborn fire effect.

## Zeraph’s Lantern

If Zeraph reveals or uses the lantern here, the ritual flames recoil briefly.

Mechanical options:

* Zeraph can use an action to make a DC 14 Religion check to suppress one brazier for 1 round.
* If he spends a spell slot while doing so, he can instead deal 10 radiant or cleansing damage to the brazier.
* Doing this increases Lantern Attention by 1.

# Scene 5 — Main Encounter: Break the Cinder Line

## Encounter Goal

Open the central street so civilians can reach the docks and the city’s firebreak can hold.

The party wins when they complete **two of three objectives**:

1. Defeat or drive off Veyr Ashmantle.
2. Disable all three ritual braziers.
3. Clear the burning barricade enough for civilians to reach the docks.

If the party completes all three, reduce City Damage by 1.

If the party only defeats enemies but ignores the braziers or barricade, they win the fight but City Damage increases by 1.

## Recommended Enemies for Level 4 Party

* Veyr Ashmantle
* 2 Ashborn Kindlers
* 1 Ashborn Censer-Bearer
* 1 Smoke Hound

If the party is struggling, remove the Smoke Hound or have one Kindler spend actions maintaining the ritual instead of attacking.

If the party is winning too easily, add 1 Ashborn Kindler from a side alley at the end of round 3.

# Battlefield Features

## Ritual Braziers

There are 3 red-burning braziers.

Each brazier:

**AC** 12
**HP** 15
**Damage Resistances** fire, poison
**Damage Vulnerabilities** cold, water-based effects at DM discretion
**Damage Immunities** psychic

A brazier can be disabled by:

* dealing 15 damage to it,
* DC 14 Religion,
* DC 14 Arcana,
* DC 14 Nature,
* dumping a large amount of water or sand onto it,
* using cleansed Haldrath water,
* using Zeraph’s lantern creatively,
* using an appropriate spell.

### Ritual Surge

At initiative count 20, active braziers surge.

* **3 active braziers:** Each creature within 5 feet of any fire, brazier, or burning barricade makes a DC 13 Dexterity save or takes 2d6 fire damage, half on success.
* **2 active braziers:** Same save, 1d6 fire damage on failure, half on success.
* **1 active brazier:** One fire hazard expands or flares. A creature near it makes a DC 13 Dexterity save or takes 1d6 fire damage.
* **0 active braziers:** The ritual fire collapses. Mundane fires remain but no longer surge unnaturally.

If at least 2 braziers remain active at the end of round 4, increase City Damage by 1.

---

## Burning Barricade

The barricade consists of overturned wagons, burning market stalls, fallen lantern posts, and collapsed beams.

It requires **3 Barricade Progress** to clear.

A PC can gain 1 progress by using an action and succeeding on:

* DC 13 Athletics to move debris,
* DC 13 Survival to find a safe path,
* DC 13 Investigation to identify the fastest structural weak point,
* DC 13 Persuasion or Intimidation to organize civilians,
* DC 14 Arcana, Nature, or Religion to disrupt ritual flame,
* an appropriate spell or class feature.

Failure complication: The PC still makes progress, but choose one:

* they take 1d6 fire or bludgeoning damage,
* the next creature moving through the route treats it as difficult terrain,
* smoke fills the opening until the end of the next round,
* a civilian group panics and must be stabilized,
* the PC’s next attack or check is made at disadvantage due to smoke and strain.

The route opens at 3 progress.

---

## Civilian Pressure

At initiative count 10, civilians press toward the barricade.

If the route is blocked, they panic, choke, or stumble.

A PC can use an action, bonus action with strong justification, or relevant spell to stabilize them with a DC 13 Persuasion, Intimidation, Performance, or Medicine check.

If civilians are ignored for 2 consecutive rounds, choose one:

* increase City Damage by 1,
* one PC must use their reaction to pull someone from fire or a civilian is badly hurt,
* the barricade gains 1 additional required progress,
* Veyr gains advantage on her next social/fear-based effect as panic spreads.

---

## Smoke Banks

Several 10-foot areas are heavily obscured.

A creature that starts its turn in heavy smoke for 2 consecutive rounds must succeed on a DC 12 Constitution save or take 1d6 poison damage.

Smoke Hounds ignore this effect.

A PC can clear one smoke bank for 1 round with:

* Gust-like magic,
* Create Water or similar suppression,
* DC 13 Nature or Survival using cloaks, blankets, or airflow,
* knocking down a burning source.

---

## Fallen Lantern Posts

A fallen lantern post can be moved or weaponized.

A PC can use an action and make a DC 13 Athletics check or attack roll.

On success, choose one:

* deal 2d6 bludgeoning damage to one creature in its path,
* gain 1 Barricade Progress,
* create half cover,
* pin a Smoke Hound or Kindler until it uses an action to escape with DC 12 Strength.

Failure complication: The post shifts but exposes the PC to heat. The PC takes 1d6 fire damage or is knocked prone.

# Stat Blocks

## Ashborn Kindler

Medium humanoid, neutral evil

**Armor Class** 13
**Hit Points** 22
**Speed** 30 ft.

STR 10 (+0)
DEX 14 (+2)
CON 12 (+1)
INT 10 (+0)
WIS 12 (+1)
CHA 10 (+0)

**Skills** Intimidation +2, Religion +3, Survival +3
**Damage Resistances** fire
**Senses** passive Perception 11
**Languages** Common, Ashborn ritual tongue or Druidic
**Challenge** 1/2

### Traits

**Fire-Walker.** The kindler ignores difficult terrain caused by ash, embers, burning debris, or smoke-blackened rubble.

**Fanatic Heat.** While within 30 feet of an active ritual brazier, the kindler has advantage on saving throws against being frightened.

**Death Burst.** When the kindler drops to 0 hit points, it releases a burst of embers. Each creature within 5 feet must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d6 fire damage.

### Actions

**Ash Knife.** Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. Hit: 1d6 + 2 piercing damage plus 1d4 fire damage.

**Cinder Flask.** Once per encounter. The kindler throws a burning flask at a point within 30 feet. Each creature within 10 feet of that point makes a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, a creature takes 2d6 fire damage. On a success, it takes half damage. Unattended flammable objects ignite.

**Ignite.** The kindler touches a flammable unattended object within 5 feet. The object catches fire.

### Motives

Desire: Spread the Prophet’s fire and draw out the lantern-bearer.
Fear: The lantern’s cleansing light, old Ashborn rites, being abandoned by the Prophet’s certainty.
Detests: Mercy, hesitation, grey ash traditions, people who call their actions murder.
Combat behavior: Kindlers light fires, threaten civilians with area hazards, and force the party to divide attention.

## Ashborn Censer-Bearer

Medium humanoid, neutral evil

**Armor Class** 12
**Hit Points** 32
**Speed** 30 ft.

STR 12 (+1)
DEX 12 (+1)
CON 13 (+1)
INT 10 (+0)
WIS 14 (+2)
CHA 12 (+1)

**Saving Throws** Wis +4
**Skills** Medicine +4, Religion +4, Intimidation +3
**Damage Resistances** fire
**Senses** passive Perception 12
**Languages** Common, Ashborn ritual tongue or Druidic
**Challenge** 1

### Traits

**Ritual Fumes.** Smoke created by the censer-bearer smells of burnt herbs, old blood, and wet ash. A creature that starts its turn in this smoke has disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks relying on smell or sight until the start of its next turn.

**Stoke the Flame.** At initiative count 20, if the censer-bearer is alive and within 30 feet of an active fire, one unattended flammable object within 30 feet begins burning. A PC can prevent this for the round by using an action to smother flames, scatter ash, or disrupt the censer with a DC 13 Athletics, Arcana, Nature, Religion, or Survival check.

### Actions

**Censer Chain.** Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. Hit: 1d6 + 2 bludgeoning damage plus 1d4 fire damage.

**Choking Censer.** Recharge 5–6. The censer-bearer fills a 10-foot-radius area within 30 feet with choking smoke until the start of its next turn. The area is heavily obscured. A creature that starts its turn in the smoke must succeed on a DC 12 Constitution saving throw or take 1d6 poison damage.

**Ashen Rebuke.** Recharge 6. One creature the censer-bearer can see within 30 feet must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or be frightened until the end of its next turn. While frightened this way, the creature must use all its movement to move away from the nearest visible fire if it can.

### Motives

Desire: Maintain ritual pressure, choke routes, and keep civilians afraid.
Fear: Losing control of the ritual flame.
Detests: Anyone who treats fire as comfort, memory, or warmth rather than command.
Combat behavior: The censer-bearer controls space with smoke and tries to make rescue actions harder.

## Veyr Ashmantle

Medium humanoid, neutral evil

**Saving Throws** Wis +4, Con +4
**Skills** Intimidation +3, Medicine +4, Religion +4
**Damage Resistances** fire
**Senses** passive Perception 12
**Languages** Common, Ashborn ritual tongue or Druidic
**Challenge** 2

### Traits

**Ritual Leader.** While Veyr is within 30 feet of an active ritual brazier, Ashborn allies within 30 feet of her have advantage on saving throws against being frightened.

**Red Ash Devotion.** The first time Veyr would be reduced to 0 hit points, she instead drops to 1 hit point and immediately moves up to half her speed without provoking opportunity attacks. Once she uses this trait, she cannot use it again.

**Fire-Walker.** Veyr ignores difficult terrain caused by ash, embers, burning debris, or smoke-blackened rubble.

### Actions

**Censer Strike.** Melee Weapon Attack: +4 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. Hit: 1d6 + 2 bludgeoning damage plus 1d4 fire damage.

**Ashen Flame.** Ranged Spell Attack: +4 to hit, range 60 ft., one target. Hit: 1d8 + 3 fire damage.

**Burning Hands.** 1/day. Veyr releases a 15-foot cone of fire. Each creature in the area makes a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, a creature takes 3d6 fire damage. On a success, it takes half damage.

**Hold Person.** 1/day. One humanoid Veyr can see within 60 feet must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or be paralyzed for up to 1 minute. The target can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success. Use sparingly.

**Command.** 2/day. One creature Veyr can see within 60 feet must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or follow a one-word command on its next turn.

### Bonus Actions

**Voice of the Pyre.** Veyr chooses one visible flame within 60 feet. The flame surges. One creature within 5 feet of that flame must make a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, the creature takes 2d6 fire damage. On a success, it takes half damage.

If the target would be a civilian, consider marking City Damage pressure instead of rolling damage.

### Reactions

**Ashen Screen.** When Veyr is hit by an attack, she can cause nearby smoke and ash to flare around her. The attack still hits, but Veyr gains half cover until the start of her next turn. She can use this reaction only while within 10 feet of smoke, fire, or a ritual brazier.

### Motives

Desire: Hold the central street until the Prophet’s message is delivered and the lantern-bearer is identified.
Fear: The ritual collapsing before it creates enough fear.
Detests: Fenrur’s teachings, Seethe’s old Ashborn restraint, Zeraph’s cleansing light, civilians who refuse to break.
Combat behavior: Veyr should not fight like a duelist. She uses fire, smoke, civilians, and terrain to make the party split their focus.

# Loot and Discoveries

## Common Loot from Ashborn Kindlers

Each Kindler may carry:

* 1d4 silver or low-value trade pieces,
* a hooked ash knife,
* fire-starting cord,
* red ash pouch,
* scorched prayer strips,
* 1 cinder flask if not used,
* blackened briar thorn charm.

### Cinder Flask

As an action, throw at a point within 30 feet. Creatures within 10 feet make a DC 12 Dexterity save. On failure, they take 2d6 fire damage, or half on success. Unattended flammable objects ignite.

Single use.

## Diluted Black Blood Vials

The party can find **1–3 vials** total during the session.

### Diluted Black Blood

A creature can use an action to administer the vial to a dying creature at 0 HP.

The creature immediately stabilizes and regains 1 HP.

Then it must make a DC 13 Constitution saving throw.

On a success, it gains one minor corruption symptom for 24 hours.

On a failure, it gains one Black Blood corruption mark or suffers one of the following:

* black veins around the wound,
* numbness in one limb,
* disadvantage on the next Wisdom saving throw against fear, hunger, rage, or darkness,
* disturbed sleep during the next long rest,
* temporary aggression under stress,
* the wound closes with black, bark-like scar tissue.

Using more than one vial on the same creature before a long rest automatically causes a failed save.

This is useful, but frightening.

## Red Ash Pouch

A small pouch of red ritual ash.

A character proficient in Religion, Nature, or Arcana can study it during a short rest.

### Use

Once, a character can scatter the ash over a fire or burned mark and learn whether the fire was:

* mundane,
* old Ashborn,
* heretical Ashborn,
* Nimyrian,
* Black Blood-related,
* magically influenced by the Prophet.

After one use, the ash is spent.

Risk: carrying red ash may make old Ashborn distrustful if discovered.

## Fenrur’s Ash Bowl

Found if the party recovers Fenrur’s remains or searches the central street after the battle.

A small black bowl used in older Ashborn rites. Inside is grey ash untouched by the Prophet’s red ash. Scratched beneath the bowl is an old Ashborn phrase:

**Remember, but do not become.**

### Use

Once per long rest, during a short rest, a creature proficient in Religion, Nature, or Insight may study ash, burn marks, or fire rites using the bowl.

They gain advantage on one check to determine whether a fire rite is:

* old Ashborn,
* heretical Ashborn,
* Nimyrian,
* corrupted by Black Blood,
* mundane,
* connected to the Prophet.

This is especially useful for Seethe.

## Cleansed Water from Haldrath

If the party collected any, or if they later return to the spring, they may have **1–3 vials**.

### Cleansed Water Vial

As an action, pour the water over a corrupted wound, object, patch of Black Blood residue, or minor briar growth.

Choose one effect:

* suppress minor Black Blood corruption for 1 hour,
* grant advantage on the next saving throw against Black Blood influence,
* deal 2d6 radiant or cleansing damage to a minor Black Blood growth,
* automatically disable one minor heretical ash mark,
* give advantage on a check to disable a ritual brazier.

It is not enough to cure a major curse or cleanse a major wound by itself.

# Scene 6 — The Docks Hold

## Situation

Once the central street opens, civilians move downhill to the docks. This is not a retreat from Grave’s End. It is the city’s emergency refuge.

The docks are safer because:

* the quays are stone,
* water is close,
* smoke disperses better in open air,
* boats can pull people out from the worst heat temporarily,
* bucket lines can move water uphill,
* healers and caretakers can gather the wounded in one place.

## Goals

Show that the party’s actions mattered.

Show that Grave’s End is damaged, but functioning.

Do not run this as another major encounter unless the session is running short and needs a small final complication.

## Optional Dock Complications

Use one if needed.

### 1. Bucket Line Fails

A frightened group drops the bucket line as fire spreads toward a lower storehouse.

Skill options: Persuasion, Athletics, Performance, Survival, Shape Water, Create Water.

Failure complication: The storehouse is saved, but supplies inside are damaged. Reduce future available mundane supplies in Grave’s End.

### 2. Smoke Victims

Several civilians collapse near the waterfront.

Skill options: Medicine, Nature, Survival, healing magic.

Failure complication: They survive, but the party spends resources or gains exhaustion from triage.

### 3. Burning Debris on the Water

A burning section of dock breaks loose and threatens moored boats.

Skill options: Athletics, Acrobatics, water magic, ranged attacks, tools.

Failure complication: The boats survive, but one is damaged and unavailable for future travel.

### 4. Ashborn Straggler

One wounded Kindler tries to reignite a lower warehouse.

Use an Ashborn Kindler at half HP.

Failure complication: The warehouse is saved, but City Damage increases by 1 or a useful NPC is wounded.

# Scene 7 — Grave’s End Survives

## Purpose

End with stability.

The party has not fixed everything. People died. Fenrur is gone. Hollow Vein is damaged. The Prophet got close enough to speak.

But Grave’s End remains.

The party does not need to run tonight.

## Description Notes

Emphasize:

* roofs still standing,
* smoke lifting,
* people patching walls,
* water lines from the docks,
* wounded wrapped in blankets,
* lanterns still burning,
* bodies being gathered,
* citizens thanking, staring, grieving, or simply surviving,
* the city taking stock rather than collapsing.

The emotional point:

Grave’s End has not escaped the fire, but it has survived it.

For the first time since Karthold, the morning does not demand that the party run.

It asks what they will do with a place that can still be saved.

# Scene 8 — Corrin Finds Them

## Situation

The party has not yet met Corrin, Riot, or Kael’ith this session.

After the fire is contained, Corrin finds them or sends someone to guide them.

Corrin knows enough to be cautious:

* the party came from Haldrath,
* the attackers were hunting a lantern-bearer,
* the party helped save Grave’s End,
* Kael’ith and Riot are already hidden,
* Riot’s survival cannot become public.

## Corrin Motives

Desire: Keep Riot hidden, preserve the rebellion’s options, protect Grave’s End, identify whether the party are useful or catastrophic.
Fear: Riot’s survival becoming public too early.
Detests: Grandstanding, sloppy heroics, miracles without plans, people who bring heat and then leave others to burn.
Approach: Guarded gratitude. Corrin should not be warm immediately, but they should recognize the party’s usefulness.

## Kael’ith Motives

Desire: Protect Riot, repair what he broke, control the story before it destroys them both.
Fear: Riot being used, killed, worshipped, or rejected.
Detests: Being seen as the one controlling Riot’s return.
Approach: Controlled, protective, visibly strained.

## Riot Motives

Desire: Matter again without becoming a false symbol.
Fear: Being remembered only as the man he was before Karthold.
Detests: Pity, being handled, being hidden while others bleed.
Approach: Ashamed, frustrated, grateful in a way that hurts him.

## Safehouse Reveal

The party is guided to Corrin’s hidden safehouse beneath an abandoned net-maker’s shop.

Inside:

* wounded rebels,
* hidden supplies,
* maps of Farshade,
* soot-streaked runners,
* Kael’ith watching the exits,
* Riot seated out of sight, one leg missing, hands marked with Riot and Ruin.

This can be the final reveal of the session if time is short.

## If Time Remains

Begin Corrin’s rebellion briefing:

* Riot was sent to Karthold and presumed dead.
* No one escapes Karthold.
* The Pale Banner splintered after his capture.
* Some remnants still remember Riot.
* The Black Ledger controls survival networks.
* The Choir of Knives is dangerous but influential.
* The Prophet is a growing threat promising punishment.
* Felmarch is where proof of Riot’s survival must eventually go.

Do not force the full briefing if the session is already at time.

A strong ending is simply the party realizing Riot is alive and hidden beneath the city they just helped save.

# Session End Options

## Ending A — Stable City

Use if the party finishes the main combat with time for aftermath.

End on Grave’s End surviving, Corrin bringing them below, and Riot revealed alive.

## Ending B — Riot Reveal Cliffhanger

Use if time is tight.

End when the party enters the safehouse and sees Riot’s tattooed fists.

## Ending C — Prophet Consequence

Use if the party revealed Zeraph clearly.

End with the Prophet elsewhere, receiving confirmation that the lantern-bearer was in Grave’s End. Do not show too much. Keep it ominous.

## Ending D — Fenrur’s Ash

Use if Seethe engages strongly with Fenrur.

End with Seethe receiving Fenrur’s Ash Bowl and understanding that the Prophet has not just attacked a city; he has declared war on the old Ashborn way.
